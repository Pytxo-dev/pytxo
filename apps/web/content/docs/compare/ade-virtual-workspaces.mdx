**ADE / virtual workspace** products (BridgeSpace, Emdash, and similar) optimize a workroom of agent terminals, kanban, and visual parallel panes. They look like “mission control” in a demo. Pytxo bets the other way: **structural telemetry and collision policy**, not terminal walls.

## Comparison

| Dimension | ADE / BridgeSpace-style | Pytxo |
|-----------|-------------------------|--------|
| What you open | A room of agent terminals | Your IDE + optional Desktop |
| What it optimizes | Visual parallel panes | Throughput, isolation, path claims |
| Product center | Terminal cinema | Hypervisor moats + Ops / Focus |
| Keys | Often platform-shaped | BYOK. Your keys. Your silicon. |
| Agents | Often product-tied | Runs the agents you already pay for |

## When an ADE fits

- You want an all-in-one visual swarm room
- Demo-first multi-pane terminals are the job
- Single-vendor or hosted sandbox workflow is enough

## When Pytxo fits

- Mixed headless CLIs under one scheduler
- Collisions are a systems problem you need solved, not painted over
- Approve-to-flush sandboxes and Galaxy HITL
- Structural Focus graph instead of sixteen scrolling PTYs

Pytxo Desktop is optional supervision — Ops, Approvals, Focus — not another ADE hero theater.
